HALLS OF RESIDENCE — OVERVIEW
=============================

1. ROLE OF HALL RESIDENCE
   Residence in a university hall is optional but strongly
   encouraged for students whose home is outside commuting
   range of the campus. Halls are academic communities under
   the authority of a Provost (a senior faculty member
   appointed by the Vice-Chancellor), and are the primary
   venue for the University's residential tutorial and
   counselling support (Office of Student Counselling,
   Section A).

3. ADMINISTRATIVE STRUCTURE (per hall)
   - Provost — head of hall (faculty member, appointed 2-year
     term, renewable once). Full authority over hall
     administration.
   - Assistant Provost — 1 per ~150 residents; assists the
     Provost and shares on-call duty.
   - House Tutor (Honorary) — assigned per block/floor from
     faculty; provides academic mentoring in residence.
   - Hall Office — clerk, accounts assistant, security
     supervisor, caretaker, cleaners, cooks (mess staff).

4. CENTRAL COORDINATION
   The Provosts report administratively to the
   Vice-Chancellor through the Proctor (Section A) for
   student-discipline matters, and to the Treasurer
   (Section A) for mess accounts (mess_management.txt).

5. PRIVATE HOUSING (MESS OUTSIDE CAMPUS)
   Students not in halls may live in private messes in the
   areas surrounding the campus. The University does not
   operate these messes but maintains a registration list;
   misbehavior from unregistered messes is still actionable
   under Section J (the University claims jurisdiction over
   enrolled students' conduct affecting the University's
   reputation).
