SUPPLEMENTARY AND IMPROVEMENT EXAMINATIONS
==========================================

1. SUPPLEMENTARY EXAMINATION (clause 6.7 — current system)
   Purpose: to clear an F/X/I grade in a course.
   - Held once per semester, in a window published with the
     academic calendar (Section K), typically 3–4 weeks after
     result publication.
   - Eligibility: the student must register and pay the
     supplementary examination fee (Section I) before the
     deadline.
   - Format: the FULL course assessment applies — a single
     examination covering the whole syllabus, replacing the
     final examination component (60 marks); the incourse
     marks earned in the original semester are carried
     forward where the department permits, or re-earned where
     attendance (75%) lapsed (attendance_policy.txt).
   - Result: the new grade is capped for record purposes —
     the maximum grade point recorded for a supplementary
     pass is 2.75 (B-) in the synthetic dataset's standing
     rule; both attempts appear on the transcript.

2. IMPROVEMENT EXAMINATION
   Purpose: to improve a PASSED grade in a course.
   - Eligibility: CGPA 2.00 or above; a course may be
     improved only ONCE; at most 30% of total programme
     credits may be improvement attempts.
   - The improvement registration replaces the grade only if
     the new grade is HIGHER; otherwise the original stands.
   - Improvement attempts appear on the transcript with the
     notation "Improvement".

3. LIMITS AND STANDING IMPACT
   - Students on probation may sit supplementary examinations
     but may not register improvement attempts.
   - Clearing a course via supplementary removes the F from
     standing computation (latest grade counts — see
     cgpa_computation.txt §5).

4. INCOMPLETE (I) CLEARANCE
   An "I" grade must be cleared at the next supplementary
   window; failure to do so converts the grade to F
   automatically (grading_policy.txt §2).

5. PRE-2015-16 ARCHIVE RULE
   Under the superseded system, backlog examinations followed
   the single-100-mark format. That rule applies to archived
   records only and is not available to current students.
